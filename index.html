Count 07 — phase one · how the money moves

Four ways to pay. Zero loose ends.

In the app

Stripe. Card typed once — then it's one tap. Apple Pay and Google Pay built in. Memberships and packs renew and top up here. Receipt lands by email automatically.

At the door · card

Your Square reader, exactly as today. Tap or insert; Square sends its own text/email receipt; the booth logs the reference so the night reconciles.

Cash

Booth records it in two taps — who, how much, which class. The app issues its own receipt by text or email if they want one.

Venmo

A QR code right on the kiosk screen. They scan, you get paid, the booth confirms and logs it. No extra apps, no fees games.

Every dollar gets a receipt. Every night reconciles. The app totals each payment type against Stripe, Square, and the cash box — and flags anything that doesn't line up before Monday.

Count 08 — phase one · judgment calls, made easy

The door works like you do.

Card failed? Your call.

A member's renewal declines — the booth sees a flag and two buttons: Admit under grace, or Decline. A loyal regular with an expired card gets waved through in one tap (logged). A stranger gaming the system gets a polite no. Never automatic, never awkward.

Free where you say so

Standing list: instructors, Carly, your helpers — flagged once by you, they check in every week with payment skipped and the reason recorded. One-night comps at the booth with a reason code. Everyone still counts in the numbers.

The waiver (with a kill switch)

First visit only: liability + 18-and-over + photo/video permission, signed by typing their name. Once per person, ever. Don't want it? One toggle turns it off and the screen disappears entirely.

The floor rule

Name and phone from everyone — including cash. No exceptions. Worst case scenario, you know exactly who was in the room. Email stays optional.

Count 10 — right behind launch

Fast-follow #1: "Boom, I'm here."

The endgame was always self check-in — a dancer walks into Wild West, taps one button on their own phone, and they're in. No booth stop, no waiting on Carly, no QR codes. The door runs itself even when she's mid-conversation across the room.

Why not night one?

Because the first Sundays are for proving the money and the roster are bulletproof with a human in the loop. Two smooth live Sundays is the trigger — then self check-in ships immediately. It's committed, not "someday."

Kept honest

Self check-in opens only near event time, uses a rotating nightly code so screenshots don't work, and still lights up Carly's list in real time. Fast for dancers, cheat-proof for you.

Count 12 — phase three · the sales engine

"I'm a salesperson first."

Phase 3 turns what's already in your head into a system any AMTD instructor can run — care first, follow-up always, nobody forgotten.

Every dancer, remembered

Goals, notes, and history on every profile: "wants to compete novice in six months," "working on West Coast," "asked about privates twice." The follow-up nudge fires so the relationship never goes cold.

Private lessons, booked in-app

Instructor profiles with open time slots — a dancer taps, books, pays. The platform takes its fair cut on lessons it originates; instructors fill their weeks without chasing.

The analytics dashboard

Attendance trends, retention curves, first-timer conversion, revenue by night — your data, in your system, with an export to GoHighLevel while it's useful. Never dependent on it.

Merch & win-backs, your hands on the dials

Discount codes you create and kill yourself — "NEW DANCER: 50% off a tee," third-visit rewards, come-back offers. No developer required to run a promotion.

"People don't care how much you know until they know how much you care." — your sales rule, now with a memory that never drops a name
Count 13 — phase four & five · the empire

Then we sell the machine.

White-label studios

Any instructor in America signs up, names their academy, sets their prices, and runs their whole business on this — ~$20/month, zero tech skills, five-minute wizard. Their data walled off from everyone else's, guaranteed.

Free tier, classy ads

A free version funded by curated dance-world ads only — boots, Fuegos, events. Clean placements, never casino junk blocking buttons.

Instructor training program

The pathway you've built — with David Miller video lessons on how to teach — as a paid track inside the app. "You have to know more than the person you're teaching" becomes a curriculum.

Personal video coaching

The premium tier: dancers upload their video, you (or your instructors) mark it up — "right here, your timing slips." A coach in their pocket, priced above membership.

The honest AI concierge

2 AM inquiry? "I'm an AI bot — I can help until an instructor reaches out in the morning." Transparent, useful, books the appointment. Your car-sales trick, done right.

The far horizon

Computer-vision posture & timing analysis · song identification with practice playlists · 24/7 badge-in studio access. Wild ideas, real scaffolding — none of it allowed to slow down Sunday.

Count 14 — how it's built · in plain english

Boring where it counts.

Money gets tested first

Anything touching payments, credits, or the door is tested before it ships — including the ugly cases: double-taps, refunds, a card declining at 4:55.

Two AIs, checked by humans

One AI builds; a second, independent AI reviews every change cold against written rules before it merges. Then it's rehearsed on a real device before a real Sunday.

A plan for the bad night

Venue Wi-Fi dies mid-rush? There's a rehearsed fallback: numbered paper roster, your Square reader keeps taking cards, and Monday morning everything reconciles into the system. No improvising.

Your data, owned by you

Everything lives in our own database — not GoHighLevel, not anyone's borrowed account. GHL becomes an export target while it's handy, never a dependency. Every override and free entry is logged: who, when, why.
